How should I think about choosing between PostgreSQL and MongoDB for a new SaaS backend?
Assistant
Bottom line 3 min read

For most SaaS backends, PostgreSQL is the safer default — you get strong relational guarantees, mature tooling, and JSONB for the genuinely schemaless parts.

  • Pick MongoDB only when your data is truly document-shaped and write-heavy at scale.
  • Postgres' JSONB handles ~80% of the flexibility people reach for Mongo for.
  • Migration cost later is much higher out of Mongo than out of Postgres.

Great question! This is one of the classic architecture decisions early-stage teams spend too long on. The honest answer is that for the vast majority of SaaS products, PostgreSQL is the stronger default, and the reasoning is less about raw performance than about the shape of the problems you'll actually hit in year two.

When Postgres wins

  • Anything relational: users, orgs, billing, audit logs, permissions
  • Anything requiring transactional integrity across multiple rows
  • Cases where your schema will evolve but the core entities are stable

When Mongo is a reasonable pick

Genuine document workloads — event streams, CMS-style content, product catalogs with wildly different attribute sets per product. The operative word is genuine: many teams reach for Mongo because their schema feels unclear, not because their data is actually document-shaped. 

// Postgres handles schemaless just fine:
CREATE TABLE events (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY,
  kind text NOT NULL,
  payload jsonb NOT NULL,
  created_at timestamptz DEFAULT now()
);

CREATE INDEX idx_events_payload ON events USING gin (payload);

The JSONB + GIN index pattern above is what most teams end up wanting from Mongo without realizing it. You get schema flexibility for the fluid parts of your data while keeping real foreign keys and transactions for everything else.
